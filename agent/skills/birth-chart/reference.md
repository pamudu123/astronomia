# The Sri Lankan birth chart and planet changes

Background facts for the site's assistant. Everything here describes **tradition and belief**, not proven fact. The scientific consensus is that astrology has no demonstrated predictive power. Say "astrologers say" or "traditionally", and never predict anything about a person. Sinhala terms appear in Sinhala script with a transliteration the first time. **(unverified)** marks points not confirmed in two independent sources.

## 1. The hadahana and the kendaraya

**What it is.** A hadahana or handahana (හඳහන) is a person's written horoscope. The kendaraya (කේන්දරය) is the chart drawing in it: where the Sun, Moon and planets stood in the zodiac at the moment and place of birth. People often use the words interchangeably. A common distinction is that the hadahana is the whole document and the kendaraya the drawing **(unverified)**. A full horoscope usually holds the main (lagna) chart, a navamsa (ninth-division) chart and a table of life periods.

**How it is traditionally made.**
- The family records the date, place and exact time of birth. One Sri Lankan guide says the ideal moment is the baby's first cry.
- An astrologer works out the planets' positions from an almanac, the panchangaya (පංචාංගය), and tables, then draws the chart. Today most charts come from software.
- Older horoscopes were written on talipot palm leaves, puskola (පුස්කොළ), with a metal stylus.
- The astrologer also suggests first letters for the child's name, based on the birth star.
- A 1979 academic study notes that the birth star was not always written on the owner's copy. An astrologer worked it out from the birth time when needed, for example for marriage matching.

**The Sri Lankan layout.**
- A square split into a 3 × 3 frame. The four corner boxes are each cut diagonally into two triangles. With the four side boxes, that makes 12 spaces, one per house.
- **Houses stay fixed and signs move.** House 1 (the lagna) is the top-centre box. The lagna sign is often named in the centre square.
- Houses are counted **anticlockwise**. Kemper (1979) states that Sinhala horoscopes run counter-clockwise, unlike South Indian ones.
- Planets are written as short Sinhala abbreviations in their house **(unverified as a fixed rule)**. The lagna may be marked ල or L.

| Style | Shape | Fixed | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sri Lankan | Square, corners split diagonally | Houses (lagna top centre) | Anticlockwise |
| North Indian | Diamonds inside a square | Houses (lagna top centre) | Anticlockwise |
| South Indian | Ring of 12 equal boxes | Signs (Pisces top left) | Clockwise |

So the Sri Lankan chart works like the North Indian one, but uses boxes and triangles instead of diamonds. Chart software lists it as a separate style.

*Site note:* the site draws house 1 at top centre and counts anticlockwise. The code marks the direction provisional until an astrologer confirms it.

## 2. The building blocks

**Rising sign: lagna (ලග්නය, lagnaya).** The sign rising on the eastern horizon at birth. It becomes house 1, and the other houses are counted from it. Astrologers treat it as the most personal point in the chart.

**Moon sign: rashi (රාශිය, rashiya).** The sign the Moon was in at birth. The Moon spends only about two and a quarter days in each sign. If the birth time is unknown, astrologers fall back on the Moon sign and birth star. In Sri Lanka, "your rashi" means the Moon sign, not the Western Sun sign.

The 12 signs: Mesha (මේෂ), Vrushabha (වෘෂභ), Mithuna (මිථුන), Kataka (කටක), Simha (සිංහ), Kanya (කන්‍යා), Thula (තුලා), Vrushchika (වෘශ්චික), Dhanu (ධනු), Makara (මකර), Kumbha (කුම්භ), Meena (මීන).

**Birth star: nakatha (නැකත) / nakshatra, and quarter: pada (පාදය, padaya).**
- The zodiac is also cut into 27 star sections of 13°20′ each. The birth star is the one the Moon was in.
- Each star has four quarters (padas) of 3°20′, so there are 108 padas.
- The birth star sets the first life period (section 4), most porondam checks (section 7) and the naming letters.
- An older system counts 28 stars by adding Abhijit. Some Sri Lankan porondam checks use it **(unverified)**.

**The nine planets: navagraha (නවග්‍රහ).**

| English | Sinhala | Sanskrit |
|---|---|---|
| Sun | Ravi (රවි) | Surya |
| Moon | Chandra (චන්ද්‍ර) | Chandra |
| Mars | Kuja (කුජ) | Mangala |
| Mercury | Budha (බුධ) | Budha |
| Jupiter | Guru (ගුරු) | Guru |
| Venus | Sikuru (සිකුරු) | Shukra |
| Saturn | Senasuru (සෙනසුරු) / Shani (ශනි) | Shani |
| Rahu | Rahu (රාහු) | Rahu |
| Ketu | Ketu (කේතු) | Ketu |

Rahu and Ketu are not physical bodies. They are the two points where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's path (the lunar nodes). They are always exactly opposite each other, and eclipses happen near them. Tradition calls them "shadow planets". Uranus, Neptune and Pluto are not used.

**The 12 houses: bhava (භාව).** Traditionally each house is one whole sign, counted from the lagna sign.

| House | Name | Traditional life areas |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Thanu (තනු) | Self, body, personality |
| 2 | Dhana (ධන) | Money, family, speech |
| 3 | Sahaja (සහජ) | Siblings, courage, short trips |
| 4 | Sukha (සුඛ) | Mother, home, property, peace of mind |
| 5 | Putra (පුත්‍ර) | Children, intelligence, past merit |
| 6 | Shathru (ශත්‍රු) | Illness, enemies, debt |
| 7 | Kalathra (කලත්‍ර) | Marriage, spouse, partners |
| 8 | Mrithyu (මෘත්‍යු) | Lifespan, sudden events, hidden matters |
| 9 | Dharma (ධර්ම) | Luck, father, religion, higher learning |
| 10 | Karma (කර්ම) | Career, status |
| 11 | Labha (ලාභ) | Income, gains, friends |
| 12 | Vyaya (ව්‍යය) | Spending, loss, life abroad, spiritual release |

The houses fall into groups:
- Angular (kendra): houses 1, 4, 7 and 10. Seen as strong.
- Trines (trikona): houses 1, 5 and 9. Seen as luckiest.
- Difficult (dusthana): houses 6, 8 and 12.

**Why an accurate birth time matters.**
- The lagna changes about every two hours on average. An hour's error can move the lagna into the next sign and shift every house.
- The Moon moves about one star a day, so the star and pada can change within hours.
- The time left in the first life period depends on the Moon's exact position, so every dasha date moves with the birth time.
- Sri Lanka's clocks changed in the past. They went to UTC+6:30 in May 1996, to UTC+6:00 that October, and back to UTC+5:30 in April 2006. Births in those years need the right offset.

Say these as tradition, briefly. They are background, not a reading of character on their own.

## 3. The sidereal zodiac and ayanamsa

**Two zodiacs.**
- Western astrology uses the **tropical** zodiac, which starts Aries at the March equinox.
- Sri Lankan and Indian astrology use the **sidereal** zodiac, which is tied to the stars.
- Earth's axis slowly wobbles (precession), so the equinox drifts against the stars by about 1 degree every 72 years. The two zodiacs matched roughly 2,000 years ago and are now about 24 degrees apart.
- That gap is the ayanamsa, ayanamshaya (අයනාංශය).

**Why Sri Lankan dates differ.** The Sun enters sidereal Mesha on about 13–14 April, the moment of the Sinhala and Tamil New Year (Aluth Avurudda). Western magazines start Aries on about 20–21 March. For most of each month, a person's Sri Lankan Sun sign is one sign earlier than the Western one. The Moon sign and lagna shift the same way.

**Which ayanamsa Sri Lanka uses.**
- **Lahiri** (also called Chitrapaksha) is by far the most common in Indian practice and the default in most Vedic software. It is named after astronomer N. C. Lahiri, who sat on India's 1952–55 Calendar Reform Committee.
- Panchanga websites for Sri Lankan cities say they use Lahiri.
- One Sri Lankan app's promotional page claims Sri Lanka officially uses a "Thirukanitham" ayanamsa **(unverified)**. "Thirukanitham" usually names a modern calculation method in Tamil almanacs, as opposed to the older Vakya method. It is not normally the name of an ayanamsa.
- No authoritative source says which ayanamsa the main Sinhala almanacs (lith) use **(unverified)**.
- Common ayanamsas differ by a few arcminutes up to about 2 degrees. That is enough to move a planet or lagna near a sign boundary, which is one reason an old hand-drawn chart can differ from a computer chart.

*Site note:* the site uses Lahiri, whole-sign houses and the mean node for Rahu and Ketu.

## 4. Life periods: the Vimshottari dasha

Astrologers divide an ideal 120-year life into nine main periods, the maha dashawa (මහ දශාව), each ruled by one planet. The order never changes. After Mercury it starts again with Ketu.

| Order | Period | Years |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ketu | 7 |
| 2 | Venus (Sikuru) | 20 |
| 3 | Sun (Ravi) | 6 |
| 4 | Moon (Chandra) | 10 |
| 5 | Mars (Kuja) | 7 |
| 6 | Rahu | 18 |
| 7 | Jupiter (Guru) | 16 |
| 8 | Saturn (Senasuru) | 19 |
| 9 | Mercury (Budha) | 17 |
| | **Total** | **120** |

**The starting period.**
- Each birth star has a ruling planet, in the table's order. Ashwini is ruled by Ketu, Bharani by Venus, Krittika by the Sun, and so on. The pattern repeats three times across the 27 stars.
- The first period belongs to the ruler of the birth star, but only part of it is left at birth. If the Moon had crossed half its star, half that planet's years remain. For example, half-way through Ashwini leaves 3.5 of Ketu's 7 years.
- After that, each period runs its full length.

**Sub-periods.** Each main period splits into nine sub-periods, called antar dashawa (අන්තර් දශාව) or bhukti. They run in the same order, starting with the main period's own planet. Each lasts (main years × sub-planet years) ÷ 120. For example, Venus inside Ketu lasts 7 × 20 ÷ 120 ≈ 1.17 years. Each sub-period can split again the same way (pratyantara).

**How astrologers read them.** No period is good or bad in itself. Astrologers judge it by how strong the ruling planet is in that chart and which houses it rules or occupies. Other dasha systems exist, but Vimshottari is the one in common use.

## 5. Planet changes (graha maru)

A graha maru (ග්‍රහ මාරු) is a planet moving into the next sign. Astrologers watch the slow planets most, because they stay long enough to colour a stretch of life. They judge the effect by which house the planet is passing through, counted from the person's **Moon sign** (sometimes the lagna). This transit reading is called gochara.

| Planet | Time per sign | Full circle | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saturn (Senasuru) | about 2.5 years | about 29.5 years | Seen as the strictest planet |
| Jupiter (Guru) | about 1 year | about 12 years | Seen as the main benefic |
| Rahu and Ketu | about 18 months | about 18.6 years | Always opposite; move backwards |

**Saturn's periods: erashtaka (ඒරාෂ්ටකය, erashtakaya).** This is the Sri Lankan name for India's sade sati.
- **Janma erashtaka (ජන්ම ඒරාෂ්ටකය).** Saturn passes through the sign before the Moon sign, then the Moon sign, then the sign after, about 2.5 years each and 7.5 years in all. Example: for a Mesha Moon, it runs from Saturn entering Meena until it leaves Vrushabha. Sri Lankan writers call it the most serious kind. The middle phase is often seen as the peak.
- **Ashtama shani (අෂ්ටම ශනි).** Saturn in the 8th sign from the Moon, about 2.5 years.
- **Lagna erashtaka (ලග්න ඒරාෂ්ටකය).** The same idea, counted from the lagna.
- Some traditions also flag Saturn in the 4th from the Moon. Sources differ on whether to count it.
- Saturn's backward loops can stretch a period beyond 7.5 years.
- Saturn returns about every 30 years, so most adults live through an erashtaka two or three times.

**What astrologers say these periods mean.**
- Sri Lankan newspaper astrologers describe testing times: delays, health worries, work or money setbacks, family strain.
- Others say the periods are not uniformly bad. Results depend on the whole chart, and some people achieve a great deal during them.
- One Sri Lankan writer says the effect is milder in some signs, such as those Saturn rules (Makara, Kumbha) or is strong in (Thula).

**Why people talk about them.**
- They can be worked out from the Moon sign alone.
- Newspapers list which signs are "in erashtaka".
- Each Saturn change moves a new group of people in or out.
- Families often respond with religious observances, such as a bodhi puja with coconut-oil lamps around a Bo tree. A study of Sri Lankan Buddhist ritual notes that astrologers often recommend this after finding unlucky planetary influences. (The assistant may describe this custom, but never recommends a remedy.)

**Jupiter and the nodes.** Jupiter is treated as the great benefic, so its yearly sign change is watched for timing good events **(unverified detail)**. Rahu and Ketu are natural malefics, like the Sun, Mars and Saturn. They change sign together, every 18 months or so.

**Retrograde: vakra (වක්‍ර), in plain words.**
- Seen from Earth, a planet sometimes seems to stop, drift backwards against the stars, then move forward again.
- It never really reverses. The effect comes from Earth overtaking it, like passing a slower car.
- Mercury does this about three times a year for around three weeks. Jupiter goes backwards about four months a year, Saturn about four and a half.
- A retrograde can carry a planet back into the sign it just left. Astrologers say a retrograde planet's effects change or are delayed.
- Rahu and Ketu always move backwards.

**Recent and upcoming changes.** For dates, always use the site's own calculation (the planet change tools); the dates below are background only. These use the Lahiri ayanamsa. Indian and Sri Lankan clocks are both UTC+5:30, so dates match. Almanacs can differ by a day, and the site's own calculation should be the reference.

- **Jupiter:** entered Simha on 31 October 2026. Turns retrograde about 13 December 2026 and is expected back in Kataka in late January 2027.
- **Rahu and Ketu:** entered Kumbha and Simha on 18 or 19 May 2025. Widely published to move into Makara (Rahu) and Kataka (Ketu) on about 5 December 2026. Some say 6 December, and the mean and true node give different dates.

## 6. The Mars check: kuja dosha (කුජ දෝෂය, kuja doshaya)

**What it is.** Kuja dosha (also mangala or bhauma dosha) is a traditional "Mars blemish" checked before marriage.
- Astrologers say it is present when Mars is in the **1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th or 12th** house. Some lists leave out the 2nd.
- Houses are counted from the lagna, and often also from the Moon and Venus.
- The traditional fear is strain in the marriage or harm to the partner.

**In Sri Lankan matching.**
- If **both** partners have it, it is traditionally treated as cancelled.
- Many conditions are said to cancel or soften it. Examples are Jupiter aspecting Mars, Mars in its own sign (Mesha or Vrushchika), and Mars in Makara or Simha.
- Sri Lankan newspaper astrologers warn against declaring it just because Mars is in the 1st or 7th house. They say hasty readings have caused needless worry and blocked marriages, and that the whole chart must be read.

## 7. Porondam: marriage matching

**Why families use it.** Before most arranged marriages, and some love marriages, families take both horoscopes to an astrologer to compare them. This is porondam (පොරොන්දම්). Matrimonial advertisements commonly ask for horoscopes. If the match is accepted, the astrologer also sets auspicious times, nakath (නැකත්), for the wedding. Astrologers themselves say the score should not decide alone, and that the full charts, kuja dosha included, should be read.

**How many factors.**
- **Sinhala practice:** twenty checks, the visi porondama (විසි පොරොන්දම). A Daily Mirror astrologer calls it the most widely used method in the country.
- **Tamil practice** (South India, many Sri Lankan Tamil families): ten checks (porutham).
- **North India:** eight checks (ashtakoota), scored out of 36. Usually 18 or more is wanted.
- A 1979 study described Sinhala astrologers using the eight-fold, 36-point method, so practice has varied.
- Most Sri Lankan sources treat **more than half** of the factors as a passing match.

**Rajju and Vedha.**
- Many Sri Lankan astrologers and matching apps treat these two as essential checks. A failure in either is a serious warning whatever the total, and some keep them out of the count.
- Rajju (රජ්ජු) splits the stars into five body-part groups. The partners' stars should be in **different** groups. It is linked to the length and stability of the marriage.
- Vedha (වේධ) lists star pairs said to strike each other. It is linked to obstacles and hardship.
- Not everyone agrees. One Sri Lankan writer says Rajju alone should not end a match, and some traditions soften it when other factors are good.

*Site note:* the site keeps Rajju and Vedha apart from the count and shows them as essential checks.

**The twenty factors.** Sri Lankan matching sites agree on these names. Naming varies a little. Some call the star-count check "Dina" instead of "Nakath", and some say "Graha Maitri" for the sign-lord check.

| Factor | Traditional meaning |
|---|---|
| Nakath (නැකත්) | Distance between the two birth stars |
| Gana (ගණ) | Temperament: divine, human or demonic |
| Mahendra (මහේන්ද්‍ර) | Star distances linked to family growth |

## Main caveats

1. **The ayanamsa used by Sinhala almanacs is unconfirmed.** Lahiri is the safe general answer and is what this site uses.
2. **The anticlockwise chart direction** rests on one academic source plus chart software.
3. **Porondam and kuja dosha rules vary** between astrologers, even though the twenty porondam names agree.
4. Many sources are newspaper astrology columns or commercial sites. They describe the tradition, not evidence that it works.
