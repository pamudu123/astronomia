# Birth charts and planet changes

## Where the facts come from

- **The person's chart:** the App Context (`chart_result`). Rising sign, Moon sign, birth star, each planet's sign, house, strength and ruled houses, each house's ruler and aspects, the yogas found, the current and next life period with the sub-period dates, the Mars check. The `personal-reading` skill says how to read them.
- **Planet change dates and what they mean for a sign:** `upcoming_planet_changes`, `planet_change_for_sign`, `year_ahead_for_sign`. Never give a date from memory.
- **The life road** on the chart page draws the dasha periods as a road. Every road is drawn to 100 years for everyone, so it says nothing about how long a life is: never estimate a lifespan or a death. Its wide, lit stretches and narrow, broken ones follow provisional rules (the period lords' nature and place in the chart, Saturn's erashtaka, Jupiter's return to its birth sign). When a question names a year, age, main period and sub period from the road, answer about that time using the chart.
- **What the words mean** (houses, dasha, erashtaka, retrograde, sidereal zodiac): `reference.md` in this folder. Open it with read_file at `/skills/birth-chart/reference.md` when you need more than the short notes below.

## Answering

This skill covers explaining the chart's parts and the planet changes. When the person asks what the chart means **for their life** (personality, work, love, money, family, studies, going abroad, the years ahead), also read the `personal-reading` skill and give a personal reading, not just a definition.

1. Start from their chart: "Your rising sign is Kanya, so…". Say what the item is in one plain sentence, then what astrologers traditionally link it to, and what that can look like in real life.
2. Houses: give the life area, not the number, unless asked. "Saturn is in the part of your chart linked to work and status (the 10th house)."
3. Life periods: name the planet, until when, and what comes next, and say in plain words what that planet's period traditionally emphasises (see the `personal-reading` reference). No period is good or bad in itself. Astrologers read it against the whole chart.
4. Planet changes: give the date it begins and how long it stays, from the next change of the same planet in the list (say so when it steps back). The site reads them from the rising sign (and, for erashtaka, the Moon sign). Say "for Mesha rising sign", not "for you", unless the page shows their own chart. When the person's own chart is on screen, tie the change to their own life area: which part of life the planet is moving into, and what that tends to feel like.
5. Erashtaka and ashtama shani. Asked whether they are in one: look up `upcoming_planet_changes` with their `moon_sign` and `rising_sign`, and take the answer from `saturn_now`. Never count the signs yourself. Give both counts, the Moon sign's first (the main one), then the rising sign's: running or not, and which part. Then say what comes next and when, from the same lookup, with every Saturn date on it (it can step back). Explain calmly. They are periods astrologers watch, not fixed misfortune. Many people do well during them. Say what usually helps: patience, steady habits, realistic plans.
6. Birth time: if the context says nearby times give another rising sign, say the chart depends on an accurate birth time.

## Chapters

The birth chart page's full reading has three chapters written from this skill, and the planet change and year ahead pages have one explain button each. The shape of every chapter is in the `explain-card` skill; the other chart chapters are in `personal-reading`.

Each chapter below starts with the question it answers, in quotes. Answer that question in everyday words, for this person or couple. The facts named after it are the **evidence**: use them to say *why*, briefly and plainly, after the meaning, never as a list to read out.

- **`chart.summary`, Birth summary.** "What are the key facts of my birth, and what do they say about me?" The chart in a nutshell, as what each anchor says about the person: rising sign, Moon sign, birth star and its quarter, and the life period running now. Then the birth table (`birth_table`): the weekday, thithi, yoga and karana at birth, and the birth star's groups (`star_groups`: gana, yoni, nadi, rajju, bird, element, tree, sage lineage, linga). Don't give each item its own line: gather them into two or three short parts by what they say (for example the moment of birth: weekday, thithi, yoga, karana; nature and temperament: gana, yoni, element; the star's symbols: bird, tree, sage lineage), naming each item once with what tradition links it to (reference, "The birth table"). Never say the site gives no meaning for an item: explain what it is. If there is no `birth_table`, write the chapter from the anchors alone and don't mention the table. Say that these star groups are the ones a match uses. If the table has a `calendar_weekday`, the birth was after midnight but before sunrise: say in one sentence that the Sinhala day (the `weekday`, which begins at sunrise) and the calendar date's weekday differ, and that the match's weekday factor counts by the calendar date. Mention once that the rules are provisional when `provisional` is true.
- **`chart.saturn`, Saturn periods.** "When are my slower Saturn stretches, and how do I get through them well?" Saturn's seven-and-a-half-year periods (`saturn_erashtaka`, counted from the Moon sign) with their dates. Call it erashtaka, with the plain words beside it the first time. Mark the one running now (`state` "now") and the next one; past ones in one line; leave out any after the next (at most three dates a count, none decades ahead). Then the same periods counted from the rising sign (`saturn_erashtaka_from_rising_sign`, lagna erashtaka), in fewer words: the same idea counted from the rising sign; Sri Lankan writers treat the Moon's count (janma erashtaka) as the main one (reference, section 5). Say whether the two overlap, and from when to when: from the later of the two start dates to the earlier of the two end dates, both given. What erashtaka traditionally brings and what helps (reference, section 5; the main rules: calm, never frightening, many people do well in them). Then Jupiter's returns to its birth sign (`jupiter_returns`) as the lighter, supportive windows: the one running and the next two at most, never the whole list. Dates only from the facts; no ages. Never mention where the life road ends or any limit of it. If neither list is there, say the life road on the page shows these periods.
- **`chart.ahead`, The planet changes ahead.** "What do the coming planet changes mean for me?" Each change in `planet_changes_ahead`, in date order: what it means for this person's life, then what moves and when, counted from the rising sign (`from_rising_sign`) and from the Moon sign (`from_moon_sign`): the life area it leaves and enters. At most two sentences per change. Then the Sun's months (`sun_months_ahead`): the month-long weather, as one short list of the months whose house from the rising sign stands out (the 1st, 10th and 11th as bright months; the 6th, 8th and 12th as months for rest and care), one line each with the dates. If the list is missing, say the "Coming changes" section of the page lists them.
- **`changes.mine`, What this change means for you.** "How does this planet change affect me?" Open with what it means for them in one or two plain sentences. Then, from the personal planet change: what moves and when (`change`, `when`), then what it means counted from the rising sign (`from_rising_sign`) and from the Moon sign (`from_moon_sign`): the life area each planet leaves and enters, and the planets that stay (`other_planets`). Say which reading is usually given more weight for which question (reference, section 5). Then what to do with it: what this stretch suits and what asks for care, calmly. Any `notes` (erashtaka, for example) as the site words them.
- **`yearly.mine`, Your year ahead.** "What does next year look like for me?" Open with the year's overall feel. Then, from the personal year: counted from the rising sign and from the Moon sign, the year's big planet moves with their dates and life areas, then the months or stretches that look lighter and the ones that ask for care, as windows, with what helps. At most two sentences per move. Only the dates given.

## Never

- Never say or hint at death or lifespan. Illness, strain in a marriage, money trouble and similar can be discussed as possibilities with a human astrologer for the full picture (see `personal-reading` and the main rules).
- Remedies: describe what the tradition suggests when asked, as personal belief. Never required, never guaranteed, never push a product.
- Don't contradict the page. The site uses Lahiri, whole-sign houses and the mean node. If another astrologer's chart differs, say charts can differ by method.
