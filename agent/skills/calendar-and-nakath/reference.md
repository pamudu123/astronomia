# The Sri Lankan almanac and auspicious times: background facts

Reference notes for the site's chat assistant (researched November 2026). Everything here is **tradition and belief**, not proven science, and Sri Lankan astrologers often disagree. Use phrases like "traditionally" or "astrologers say".

---

## 1. Nakath (නැකත්, auspicious times)

**What they are.** A nakatha (නැකත, singular) is a moment astrologers judge lucky for beginning something. The word comes from the lunar mansions (section 2). Astrologers choose it from the Moon and planet positions in the almanac, and for personal events they also check the person's horoscope.

**How people use them.** Many Sri Lankans, especially Sinhala Buddhists and Hindus, ask an astrologer for a nakatha before an important start:

- **Weddings.** The astrologer matches the couple's horoscopes, then sets times for building the poruwa (පෝරුව, wedding platform) and for the couple stepping on and off it.
- **Houses.** Rural Sinhala families can see a house built in the wrong place or at the wrong time as a cause of misfortune. People also check their yearly forecast, palapala (පලාපල), before moving house or a big journey.
- **Business and daily life.** Believers time business launches, and sometimes everyday tasks, by nakath.
- **Buying a vehicle or starting a new job.** Widely said to be common, but no written source was found (unverified).

**The New Year nakath.** The best-known nakath are for the Sinhala and Tamil New Year (අලුත් අවුරුද්ද, Aluth Avurudda) in April, when the Sun moves from Pisces (මීන, Meena) into Aries (මේෂ, Mesha). Between the old and new year is a gap of about 12 hours 48 minutes, the nonagathaya (නොනගතය) or punya kalaya (පුණ්‍ය කාලය), set aside for religious activity. Then the whole country lights the hearth, boils milk, eats, trades, anoints oil and returns to work at the same set times.

**Who sets them.**
- Personal events: a family astrologer (in the past, often the village temple's chief monk).
- The New Year: since about 1992 the government has issued one national list, the Avurudu Nakath Seettuwa (අවුරුදු නැකත් සීට්ටුව). In Kandyan times a royal astrologer, the Nekath Rala (නැකැත් රාළ), set times for the Temple of the Tooth.
- It is contested. In 2024 the State Nakath Committee and a rival astrologers' group issued different New Year times, and the committee was later disbanded. For 2026 the Ministry of Buddhasasana, Religious and Cultural Affairs agreed one list with the authors of two leading almanacs (the Epa and Thirasara panchanga lithas).
- Tamil Hindus use their own almanacs (Vakya and Thirukanitha panchangams), so some of their times differ.

**How a nakatha is written.** Date and time to the minute, a direction to face, a colour to wear, and sometimes extra details (for example, leaves to place on the head and at the feet when anointing oil). Example from the 2026 list: 14 April, light the hearth at 10.51 a.m., wearing red, facing south.

**Other views.** Some Sri Lankan writers argue astrology lacks evidence and that Buddhism does not depend on lucky times. Many still value the New Year nakath as a custom that unites the country.

## 2. The litha (ලිත) and its five elements

The **litha** is the Sinhala almanac, the local form of the panchanga (පංචාංගය, panchangaya, "five limbs"). It is printed before each April New Year and lists, day by day, planet positions, the five elements below, sunrise and sunset, Rahu kalaya and yearly forecasts.

| Element | Sinhala | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Thithi | තිථිය (thithiya) | Lunar day: ends each time the Moon gains 12° on the Sun. 30 per lunar month (15 waxing, 15 waning); not the same length as a calendar day. |
| Nakatha | නැකත (nakatha) | Which of 27 star groups (13° 20′ each) the Moon is passing through. |
| Yoga | යෝගය (yogaya) | One of 27 values from adding the Sun's and Moon's positions. |
| Karana | කරණය (karanaya) | Half a thithi; 11 kinds. |
| Weekday | වාරය (varaya) | Day of the week, each linked to a planet (Monday the Moon, Tuesday Mars, and so on). |

**Thithi.** Full-moon day is pasaloswaka (පසළොස්වක), new-moon day amavaka (අමාවක), the eighth day atawaka (අටවක). Indian tradition calls the 4th, 9th and 14th "rikta" (empty) days, poor for new starts; the new-moon day is also often avoided (unverified for Sri Lankan practice).

**Nakatha and birth stars.** The same 27 nakath are the birth stars: your upan nakatha (උපන් නැකත) is where the Moon was when you were born. The Sinhala list begins අස්විද (Asvida), බෙරණ (Berana), කැති (Kethi), රෙහෙණ (Rehena), මුවසිරස (Muvasirasa), අද (Ada), පුනාවස (Punavasa), පුෂ (Pusha), අස්ලිය (Asliya), මා (Maa), and continues Puvapal, Uthrapal, Hatha, Sitha, Saa, Visa, Anura, Deta, Mula, Puvasala, Uthrasala, Suvana, Denata, Siyavasa, Puvaputupa, Uthraputupa, Revathi (spellings after Maa unverified). **Always use the site's own star names**, from the page or the tools, not this list. For personal timing, astrologers count from the birth star to the day's star; some counts are thought favourable, others harmful.

**Yoga and karana.** Indian sources treat the Vyatipata and Vaidhriti yogas as the worst for beginnings, and avoid the Vishti (Bhadra) karana for weddings, housewarmings, journeys and openings. Sri Lankan use of these rules is unverified.

**In practice**, astrologers weigh all five together with the rising sign (ලග්නය, lagnaya) and the person's chart. Different almanacs and methods mean two astrologers can give different times.

## 3. Rahu kalaya (රාහු කාලය)

**What it is.** A daily period of about 90 minutes traditionally treated as unlucky for starting anything new. Rahu (රාහු) is the "shadow planet" of Indian astrology, which in myth swallows the Sun or Moon to cause eclipses.

**How it is worked out.** Take that day's local sunrise and sunset, split the daylight into eight equal parts, and pick the part for the weekday. The first part never holds Rahu kalaya.

| Day | Part | Rough time (6 a.m. to 6 p.m. day) |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | 2nd | 7.30–9.00 a.m. |
| Saturday | 3rd | 9.00–10.30 a.m. |
| Friday | 4th | 10.30 a.m.–12.00 noon |
| Wednesday | 5th | 12.00–1.30 p.m. |
| Thursday | 6th | 1.30–3.00 p.m. |
| Tuesday | 7th | 3.00–4.30 p.m. |
| Sunday | 8th | 4.30–6.00 p.m. |

Exact times shift with date and place. Sri Lanka's days stay near 12 hours all year, so these rough times are usually close.

**Why people avoid it.** Astrologers advise not to begin important things (a wedding, journey, deal or house move) during it; work already under way can continue. Sinhala almanacs and Sri Lankan almanac sellers list it daily.

**Yamaganda and gulika.** Indian almanacs list these two further daily periods, and some Indian-run Sinhala-language websites show them. It could not be confirmed that mainstream Sri Lankan lithas use them (unverified), so do not present them as Sri Lankan custom.

**Other Indian almanac times.** Sinhala-language sites such as lankaastrology.com also explain these. The site calculates none of them. Say what each is, briefly, if asked:

- **Abhijit muhurtha:** about 48 minutes around local midday, held to be good for most beginnings (some say not for travel south).
- **Choghadiya:** the day and the night each split into eight parts, each rated good, neutral or bad (a North Indian system).
- **Hora:** each hour of the day ruled by a planet in a fixed order, starting with the weekday's planet at sunrise.
- **Panchapakshi:** a Tamil system in which five birds, tied to the birth star, take turns ruling the day's activities.

Some of these pages describe yamaganda in frightening terms (accidents, death). Never repeat that. At most say tradition avoids starting new things then.

## 4. Poya days (පෝය)

**What they are.** Poya is the full-moon day of each lunar month (from Pali "uposatha", observance day). Every full-moon poya is a public and bank holiday; selling alcohol and meat is banned and many shops close. Many Buddhists wear white and spend the day at the temple keeping sil (සිල්), usually the eight precepts (අටසිල්, atasil), listening to sermons and meditating.

**Dates.** A government-appointed Poya Committee fixes them for the official calendar. A poya can fall a day either side of the astronomical full moon. (From 1966 to 1971, poya days at each quarter of the moon replaced Saturday and Sunday as the weekend; the Holidays Act of 1971 restored Sunday.)

**The twelve poya days** (usual Gregorian month in brackets):

| Poya | Traditionally commemorates |
|---|---|
| **Duruthu** දුරුතු (Dec/Jan) | The Buddha's first visit to Sri Lanka, at Mahiyangana, to end a Yakkha–Naga conflict. Also the Kelaniya Duruthu perahera (පෙරහැර, procession). |
| **Navam** නවම් (Jan/Feb) | Sariputta and Moggallana appointed chief disciples; some add the Buddha announcing his coming passing. Gangaramaya's Navam Perahera. |
| **Medin** මැදින් (Feb/Mar) | The Buddha's first return to Kapilavastu to visit his father, King Suddhodana. |
| **Bak** බක් (Mar/Apr) | The second visit, to Nagadeepa, settling two Naga kings' dispute over a gem throne. |
| **Vesak** වෙසක් (Apr/May) | The Buddha's birth, enlightenment and passing; locally also his third visit (Kelaniya) and Prince Vijaya's arrival. |
| **Poson** පොසොන් (May/Jun) | Arahat Mahinda brings Buddhism, meeting King Devanampiyatissa at Mihintale. |
| **Esala** ඇසළ (Jun/Jul) | The first sermon, plus the conception and renunciation; monks start the rains retreat (වස්, vas); the Kandy Esala Perahera. |
| **Nikini** නිකිණි (Jul/Aug) | The First Buddhist Council at Rajagaha; a late rains retreat may begin. |
| **Binara** බිනර (Aug/Sep) | Founding of the order of nuns (භික්ෂුණී, bhikkhuni) with Maha Pajapati Gotami. |
| **Vap** වප් (Sep/Oct) | End of the rains retreat and start of the Katina (කඨින) robe offerings; many say the Buddha returned from Tavatimsa heaven after teaching his mother. |
| **Il** ඉල් (Oct/Nov) | The Buddha sends out the first sixty arahats; Maitreya's assurance of future Buddhahood; end of the Katina month. |
| **Unduvap** උඳුවප් (Nov/Dec) | Theri Sanghamitta brings the Bodhi sapling to Anuradhapura and starts the nuns' order in Sri Lanka. |

Sources differ in places (one Buddhist centre links Maitreya to Vap rather than Il).

**Adhi poya (අධි, "extra").** Twelve lunar months are about 354 days, roughly 11 short of a solar year. So about every two to three years (about 7 times in 19 years) a 13th month is added. It takes the name of the month it doubles, prefixed "Adhi": Adhi Vesak (2010), Adhi Esala (2023), Adhi Poson (30 May 2026). Which full moon is the "extra" one can be disputed. In 2026 the chief monks argued 30 May was the true Vesak because the Moon was then in the Visa nakatha, and the State Vesak Festival was held on 30 May, though the calendar called it Adhi Poson.

## 5. Sinhala months and the Gregorian calendar

The traditional calendar is **lunisolar**: months of 29 or 30 days follow the Moon, and adhi months keep the year in step with the Sun. Each month is named after its full moon; the Pali names come from the star group near that full moon (Vesak goes with the Visa nakatha).

| Sinhala | Pali | Rough match |
|---|---|---|
| Bak බක් | Citta | Mar–Apr |
| Vesak වෙසක් | Vesakha | Apr–May |
| Poson පොසොන් | Jettha | May–Jun |
| Esala ඇසළ | Asalha | Jun–Jul |
| Nikini නිකිණි | Savana | Jul–Aug |
| Binara බිනර | Potthapada | Aug–Sep |
| Vap වප් | Assayuja | Sep–Oct |
| Il ඉල් | Kattika | Oct–Nov |
| Unduvap උඳුවප් | Magasira | Nov–Dec |
| Duruthu දුරුතු | Phussa | Dec–Jan |
| Navam නවම් | Magha | Jan–Feb |
| Medin මැදින් | Phagguna | Feb–Mar |

- Each month falls about 11 days earlier in the Gregorian calendar than the year before, then jumps about 19 days later after an adhi month. So Vesak poya can be anywhere from late April to late May.
- The **New Year is solar**: it follows the Sun's entry into Aries, so it stays at 13 or 14 April, within the month of Bak.
- Example dates: 2026 Il Poya 24 November, Unduvap 23 December; 2027 Duruthu 22 January. Always check the official calendar.

## 6. Directions (දිශා) and colours in nakath

**Directions.** Most nakath name a direction to face: east (නැගෙනහිර, negenahira), west (බටහිර, batahira), north (උතුර, uthura) or south (දකුණ, dakuna). The 2026 list said south for lighting the hearth and east for anointing oil; 2025 said north for leaving for work. At weddings the poruwa is placed facing a direction believed to bring prosperity, and the couple face a set direction as they step on, on the belief that starting a task facing the right way brings better results. No reliable account was found of how astrologers choose the direction (unverified). Indian almanacs have weekday "disha shool" rules on directions to avoid when travelling; No source confirms a Sri Lankan version (unverified).

**Colours.** Each nakatha usually names a colour, which changes by year and ritual. Astrologers link colours to the nine planets (නවග්‍රහ, navagraha): Moon white or pearl, Venus white, Mars red, Mercury green, Jupiter gold or yellow, Sun gold. In published New Year lists the colour usually matches the planet ruling that weekday: red on Tuesdays (Mars) in 2015 and 2026, green on Wednesdays (Mercury) in 2025 and 2026, gold on a Thursday (Jupiter) and pearl-white on a Monday (Moon) in 2025. This is an observation from the lists, not a rule stated by any source, and not every item fits (unverified).

**Suggested answer:** "The direction and colour are part of the tradition. Astrologers choose them to line the act up with the planets of that moment. Follow the official list or your own astrologer, as practice varies."

## Caveats for the assistant

- Present nakath, Rahu kalaya and lucky or unlucky days as tradition, never fact, and note that astrologers differ.
- The site works out wedding and first letters times from its own rules (under a match result, and on the first letters page). For other personal nakath, point to a qualified astrologer.
- Poya and New Year dates change yearly; point to the government calendar.
- Thithi, yoga and karana details come mostly from Indian panchanga sources; Sri Lankan practice may differ.
