# The old horoscope search: background

How the site turns a paper kendara back into possible births (docs/13). Plain-words background for explaining the results; the results themselves always come from the page.

## The drawing

A Sri Lankan kendara puts the rising sign (lagna) in the top centre box, and the houses run anticlockwise from there. So a drawing is the rising sign plus the house of each planet. The navamsha kendara, when the paper has one, is read the same way.

## Why one drawing fits several times

- **The fast parts:** the rising sign changes about every two hours, and the Moon moves to a new sign every two and a half days. So a drawing often fits once a day for two or three days in a row.
- **The slow parts:** Saturn takes about 29 years to go round, Jupiter about 12, Rahu and Ketu about 18. The same mix of slow planets can come back decades apart, so the same drawing can fit in, say, the 1940s and the 1990s.
- **Missing planets:** a planet left out of the drawing isn't checked, so a drawing with few planets fits many more times. The search stops at 50 windows.
- **The navamsha:** when the paper has the navamsha boxes too, each window narrows from about two hours to about 13 minutes, and usually one window is left.

## Exact and near

- **Exact:** every planet and the rising sign sit inside their boxes by the site's method.
- **Near:** something fits only within the edge margin, 2 degrees for signs and 1 degree for navamsha boxes. The site names what is near.
- **Why the margin:** older horoscopes were not always drawn with the site's method. Other ayanamsas (Raman, Thirukanitam, KP), older almanacs based on the Surya Siddhanta, and the true node instead of the mean node can each move a planet by a degree or two. A near window is still a real candidate; it means "check that box on the paper".

## Narrowing the results

- **A year range:** the most powerful filter. Family memory (an elder's age, a school leaving year, a well-known event at the time of birth) usually gives a decade or better.
- **A known date:** if the date is known but not the time, the search keeps that local day only.
- **The birth star:** often written on the paper or remembered by the family.
- **The navamsha boxes:** if the paper has them, enter them.
- **Checking the copy:** most "impossible" drawings are copying slips. Rahu and Ketu must be in opposite boxes; Mercury is never more than one sign from the Sun, Venus never more than two.

## What to do with a window

- **Open as a hadahan:** the full chart for the middle of the window, with the birth time check that shows what may change inside the window.
- **Use in a match:** as the bride or the groom, with full birth details.
- **Long windows:** a window longer than half an hour means the rising sign may change inside it; the hadahan's birth time check shows how.

## Limits

- Births from 1900 to today only.
- A birth very near the edge of a window may fall just outside it, if the paper was drawn with another method.
- Nothing typed into the drawing is stored or logged.
